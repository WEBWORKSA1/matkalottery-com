Lottery tax in India: what you really take home

By the MatkaLottery.com editorial team. Last checked 12 November 2026. 7 minute read. General information, not tax advice.

In one paragraph: lottery winnings are taxed at a flat 30% with no deductions, plus a surcharge on that tax if your total income tops ₹50 lakh, plus a 4% cess. The payer deducts 30% TDS on winnings above ₹10,000; you pay any surcharge and cess yourself. On a Kerala ticket, a 10% agent commission comes off first. From 1 April 2026 the sections were renumbered under the Income-tax Act, 2025, but the rules did not change.

The flat 30% rule

Winnings from lotteries, crossword puzzles, game shows, card games and other games of any kind are taxed at a special flat rate of 30% — Section 115BB of the 1961 Act, carried into Section 194 of the Income-tax Act, 2025. Three things make it harsher than ordinary income:

  • It applies whatever your income slab, even if your other income is nil.
  • No basic exemption and no deductions such as 80C or 80D can be set against it.
  • You cannot deduct what you spent on tickets.

Surcharge and cess

A surcharge is added to the tax when your total income for the year — winnings plus everything else — crosses these thresholds. A 4% health and education cess is then charged on tax plus surcharge.

Up to ₹50 lakhNilNil31.2%
₹50 lakh – ₹1 crore10%10%34.32%
₹1 crore – ₹2 crore15%15%35.88%
₹2 crore – ₹5 crore25%25%39%

Marginal relief can trim the surcharge for incomes just above a threshold. The new regime is the default unless you opt out.

TDS: what is deducted before you’re paid

When winnings exceed ₹10,000, the payer must deduct 30% tax at source before paying you — Section 194B of the 1961 Act, now Section 393(3) of the 2025 Act. Smaller prizes are paid in full, but the 30% tax still applies when you file your return.

For prizes given in kind — a car or a phone — the law allows a genuine company to collect the TDS from you before handing the prize over. Fraudsters copy this rule. Before paying anything, verify the company and the contest independently, and never pay to a personal account or UPI ID.

Kerala: commission comes off first

Kerala deducts a 10% agent commission from the prize, then TDS on the rest. Surcharge and cess are not usually deducted by the department, so a big winner still owes them.

Worked example: ₹1 crore weekly first prize

Prize₹1,00,00,000
Agent commission (10%)− ₹10,00,000
Taxable winnings₹90,00,000
Tax at 30% (deducted as TDS)− ₹27,00,000
Surcharge, 10% of tax− ₹2,70,000
Cess, 4% of tax + surcharge− ₹1,18,800
You keep₹59,11,200

Worked example: ₹25 crore bumper

After the ₹2.5 crore commission, ₹22.5 crore is taxable and TDS takes ₹6.75 crore. Then the regime matters:

New regime (25% surcharge cap)Old regime (37% surcharge)
Surcharge₹1,68,75,000₹2,49,75,000
Cess₹33,75,000₹36,99,000
Total tax₹8,77,50,000₹9,61,74,000

That is an ₹84 lakh difference from one choice — a good reason to speak to a chartered accountant before you file.

Paying the balance

  1. Collect the TDS certificate from the payer and check that the TDS shows in your annual tax statement.
  2. Pay the remaining surcharge and cess as advance tax in the year you win. Late payment can attract interest.
  3. Report the winnings as “income from other sources” in your return. Lottery winners generally cannot use the simplest ITR form.
  4. Keep the ticket copy, claim papers and bank credit advice for at least eight years.

Mistakes that cost winners money

  • Planning around the advertised prize instead of the take-home figure.
  • Spending before the surcharge and cess are paid.
  • Gifting or lending large sums to family without advice — there can be tax consequences on the income those sums earn.
  • Paying a stranger who says they can “clear the tax” and release the prize faster.
